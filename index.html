AI Agents: Exploring What We Choose to Keep, Hand Off, and Why

AI has moved past chatbots. Today's tools take a goal and work toward it across multiple steps, making decisions and using tools on their own. They build presentations, write code, analyze data, draft strategies, and deploy working apps, often in minutes. This technology is already reshaping our work, and increasingly it is reshaping how we connect with one another, drafting our messages, filling in for the friend we used to ask, and shaping the decisions we once sat with on our own. 03 — Interview

Interview a Young Person

Before we gather at Lone Rock, please spend 30–45 minutes interviewing someone aged 13–22. The goal is to understand how they're experiencing connection today — what their most meaningful relationships actually look and feel like, what gets in the way, and where technology fits into all of it.

Below is guidance to riff on. It likely has more questions than you'll get to, so use it as a starting point (and consider those with a ★ as potential priority questions).

When you are done, we'll ask you to prioritize your top 3–5 quotes — most surprising, insightful, important to share back with the group.

Tips

Take notes + capture quotes  ·  Invite specific stories  ·  If you get a general answer, try: "Can you think of a specific time that happened?"  ·  Ask "why" + "can you say more"  ·  Employ wait time + silence

Opening

"I'm part of a group thinking about how young people experience connection and build relationships, and the ways technology shapes that. I just want to learn from your personal experience. I won't share your name, so I'd encourage you to be as honest as you're comfortable with."

1. Close Friendships

  • Tell me about one of your closest friends. What do you appreciate about your friendship?
  • How do you normally connect with them — in person, online, or both?
  • ★ Can you tell me about a specific recent moment with one of them that felt really good? What were you doing? What made it feel that way?
  • Has there been a time things got tricky between you — a disagreement or misunderstanding? How did you handle it?
  • Do you feel like you have people you can "be real" with? What makes you feel like you can be?
  • Would you feel comfortable asking them for help if you really needed it? What kind of help?

2. Outer Circle

  • What about people who aren't your inner circle but are still part of your life — what do those friendships give you that's different?
  • What does friendship look like in online spaces for you and people you know? How is that different than in-person?

3. Adults in Your Life

  • ★ Who are the adults you feel closest to? How do they show you that you matter to them?
  • How comfortable do you feel asking adults for help — and what makes the difference between an adult you'd go to and one you wouldn't?
  • What kinds of things would you feel okay talking to them about? What would you not bring to them — and what makes those topics feel off-limits? (If short answer: Can you think of a time you considered going to an adult but decided not to? What happened?)

4. Navigating Hard Social Moments

  • ★ Tell me about a time you had to navigate something hard socially — a crush, a breakup, a fight with a friend or family member, anything that felt complicated.
  • How did you figure out what to do? Did you talk to anyone?
  • Who would you feel comfortable going to for something like that — and who wouldn't you go to? What makes the difference?

5. AI and Connection

Transition: "I want to shift to something related. I've been reading about the ways people are starting to use AI for personal stuff — like talking through something hard, getting advice on a social situation, or help writing a message."

  • Have you heard about people using AI that way? Where?
  • ★ Have you ever tried it yourself? Tell me about that.
    • If yes: What prompted you to try it? What was it like? Would you do it again?
    • If no: Is it something you've thought about? What appeals to you or puts you off?
  • When you think about going to AI versus going to a person for that kind of support — how do you think about the difference? Is one better for certain things?
  • Do you ever talk about this with your friends or the adults in your life?
    • If yes: What was that conversation like?
    • If no: Why do you think that is?

6. Looking Forward

  • ★ What do you think are the biggest obstacles your generation faces when it comes to building meaningful connections?
  • Fast forward 5–10 years — what do you want your relationships and community to look like? What would it take to get there?
  • ★ What do you wish adults knew about how young people experience connection today?

Optional Extension: Map Their Connection for a Day

On a piece of paper, have them create a line graph that charts their feeling of connectedness to others from a specific day this week. Once they do that, invite them to label some of the peaks (high points) and valleys (low moments). Then ask them to note any technologies/apps they were using across the day, by circling the moments where tech was most present.

Discuss the chart:

  • Tell me more about the peaks — What are you doing? Who are you with? How does it feel?
  • Tell me about the valleys — what are you doing? Where are you? Who are you with? How does it feel?
  • When in this map are you using tech? When does that lead to better or worse feelings of connection?
  • When you look at the valleys vs the peaks, what do you notice about when you feel more connected and less?
  • I notice ___, can you say more about that.
